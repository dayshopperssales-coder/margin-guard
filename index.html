The two mistakes that quietly cost money

1. Using the supplier's suggested retail as your cost. Suppliers show a strikethrough "compare at" that is routinely ~2× the real price. On a product that costs $6.50, a naive tool reads the $12.99 retail as cost and calls the margin thin. It is not thin — your margin is roughly double what that figure implies.

2. Folding freight into the product price. Padding the product price to "cover shipping" hides the real per-order economics and inflates your ad cost, because every extra dollar of price also raises what you pay in payment fees. Pass freight through separately and keep the product margin honest.

Notes on this calculator

  • Freight is counted once per unit shipped, not once per order.
  • Returns are modelled as a straight percentage haircut on contribution — a simplification, since a returned unit usually returns some cost with it. Treat it as a floor, not a forecast.
  • Taxes, duties and IOSS are excluded. In the US marketplace they generally fall on the buyer.
  • No data leaves your browser. This page has no analytics and no backend.
